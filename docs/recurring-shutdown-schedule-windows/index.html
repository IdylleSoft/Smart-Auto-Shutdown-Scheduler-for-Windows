WINDOWS 11 & WINDOWS 10 GUIDE

Make a shutdown routine
that follows your real week.

A recurring shutdown schedule does not have to mean every night. Select the days that fit your workweek, adapt it to a changing shift pattern, and cancel it whenever an exception matters more than the routine.

Use Routine for repeating patterns

Open Routine when the same action should run more than once. Choose the action, choose the time, and select the days. This keeps a recurring plan separate from a one-time Schedule, so you can immediately see whether a shutdown will repeat next week.

The best rule is the narrowest one that fits your normal pattern. Rather than choosing every day by habit, select only the days when the computer really should finish its session at that time.

Use a one-time Schedule for a single date: Routine is for repeating days. When you only need one special action, a separate one-time plan is clearer and easier to review.

Weekdays and selected days

Most routines fall into one of three patterns:

  1. Weekdays: select Monday through Friday for a desk PC that should end its workday consistently.
  2. Selected days: choose only the evenings that match classes, appointments, game nights, or a part-time work pattern.
  3. Every day: use this only when the household or device truly follows the same overnight routine every day.

After creating the routine, look at the selected day buttons once more before starting it. Most recurring-schedule mistakes come from a day being included by accident, not from the time itself.

Handle changing shifts deliberately

A weekly routine works best for a stable pattern. If your shifts rotate, create the routine for the schedule you are currently following and revisit it when the next rotation begins. Keeping one old, vague rule active is more likely to cause a surprise than taking a moment to update it.

For a temporary late shift, overnight task, or a week that does not follow your usual pattern, cancel the active routine and use a one-time Schedule when you know the appropriate date and time. Restore the regular routine when the exception is over.

Plan for exceptions before they happen

Holidays, travel, long backups, overnight downloads, and shared-device use are all good reasons to stop a routine temporarily. Do not rely on remembering it at the last minute: cancel the routine as soon as the exception is known.

Keep the pre-action warning enabled for shutdown, restart, hibernate, or sign-out actions. If the routine is still active when circumstances change, the warning provides one last chance to cancel it before the PC acts.

Keep recurring rules easy to audit

Review the action, time, and selected days whenever your work or home schedule changes. A routine should be understandable at a glance. If it is not, cancel it and rebuild a simpler one that describes the current pattern.

The optional desktop widget can keep the next action visible while the main app is closed, making it easier to notice that a routine is scheduled for later that day.

Recurring shutdown schedule FAQ

Can I schedule a shutdown every weekday?

Yes. Create a Routine, choose Shut Down, set the time, and select Monday through Friday.

Can I choose only certain days?

Yes. Select exactly the days when the routine should run instead of using an all-days schedule.

How should I handle an unusual work week or shift?

Cancel the active routine while the exception applies, then use a one-time Schedule for a known date and time if needed. Restore or update the routine afterward.

Can I stop a recurring shutdown routine?

Yes. Cancel the active Routine before its next action. Review it again when your normal schedule resumes.
