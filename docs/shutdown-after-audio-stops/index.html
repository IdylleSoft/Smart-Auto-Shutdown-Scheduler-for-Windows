WINDOWS 11 & WINDOWS 10 GUIDE

When the sound ends,
let the room go quiet.

For music, podcasts, films, and presentations, the end of audio can be a more natural signal than a clock. An Audio Monitor keeps the PC awake while sound is playing, then starts your chosen action after it stops.

How an Audio Monitor works

Auto Shutdown & Scheduler watches whether audio is playing. When the audio ends, it can continue to the action you configured after the monitor’s conditions are met. This is useful when you do not know exactly how long an album, podcast episode, playlist, film, or presentation will last.

The rule is based on sound activity, not on whether a particular media player stays open. That makes it convenient across different players, but it also means other system audio can affect the result.

Use a grace period: short silent moments can occur in films, playlists, and presentations. Choose settings that wait long enough to distinguish an ending from a pause.

Choose what happens next

Before enabling the monitor, decide what the end of playback should mean for your PC:

  1. Sleep: a comfortable choice for music or podcasts when you want to resume quickly later.
  2. Lock: ideal after a presentation or shared-device session when you want privacy without closing work.
  3. Shut Down: better for the end of the day, when you have already saved everything important.

Restart, Hibernate, and Sign Out can also be selected where they better match your routine. For actions that can close applications, save work first and keep a warning enabled.

Set it up and test a real session

Open Audio Monitor, choose the action, then configure how long the audio should remain inactive before it runs. Start with a longer period than you think you need. Play a familiar track or short video and observe how the monitor behaves once playback finishes.

Testing reveals whether notifications, browser tabs, meeting apps, or other software are generating sound in the background. If they are, close or silence them before relying on the monitor for a bedtime or end-of-day automation.

Avoid false endings

Audio silence is not always a finished session. A film can have a quiet scene, a streaming service can buffer, and a playlist can pause between tracks. This is why the waiting duration matters more than a rapid reaction to a single moment of silence.

For a known duration, a standard timer may be simpler. For an unpredictable listening or viewing session, the Audio Monitor is more natural—as long as you give it enough time and leave yourself a final cancellation option.

Audio shutdown FAQ

Can my PC shut down when music ends?

Yes. Use Audio Monitor, select Shut Down, and set a suitable period for audio inactivity before the action runs.

Will it work after a film, podcast, or presentation?

It can. The monitor responds to audio activity rather than a specific player, so it is useful for many kinds of playback.

Could silence in the middle of a film trigger the action?

It could if the inactivity period is too short. Use a longer grace period and test it with the kind of content you normally play.

What if another app makes a sound?

System notifications or other applications can keep audio active. Close or silence background apps when you need a reliable end-of-playback rule.
